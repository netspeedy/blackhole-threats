blackhole-threats documentation · v1.1.9

Threat feeds in.
Blackhole routes out.

blackhole-threats reads threat feeds from files and URLs, pulls out the IPv4 and IPv6 prefixes, summarises them and announces them to your BGP peers with a community such as 64520:666. Your routers match the community and drop the traffic. When a feed fails, its routes stay as they were.

Read the quickstartHow it works
$ docker pull ghcr.io/netspeedy/blackhole-threats:latest
# the quickstart's run, startup lines left out
$ blackhole-threats -conf lab.yaml -once

time=2026-11-05T04:38:15Z level=info msg="Parsed threat feed" prefixes_dropped=0 prefixes_read=4 source=./local-feed.txt
time=2026-11-05T04:38:15Z level=info msg="Prepared community routes" community=64520:1200 feed_count=1 prefixes_dropped=0 prefixes_read=4 prefixes_summarized=3
time=2026-11-05T04:38:15Z level=info msg="Refresh completed" active_routes=3 announced=3 duration_ms=2 withdrawn=0
time=2026-11-05T04:38:15Z level=info msg="Completed single refresh cycle"
# examples/blackhole-threats.yaml, shortened
gobgp:
  global:
    config:
      as: 64520
      routerid: "198.51.100.10"
  neighbors:
    - config:
        neighboraddress: "198.51.100.1"
        peeras: 64520
    - config:
        neighboraddress: "2001:db8:10::1"
        peeras: 64520

feeds:
  - url: https://team-cymru.org/Services/Bogons/fullbogons-ipv4.txt
    community: 64520:1101
  - url: https://team-cymru.org/Services/Bogons/fullbogons-ipv6.txt
    community: 64520:1101
  - url: https://www.spamhaus.org/drop/drop_v4.json
    community: 64520:1102
# the Debian package installs the example config here
$ blackhole-threats -conf /etc/blackhole-threats.yaml -check-config

configuration OK: 11 feed(s)

blackhole-threats

Turns threat feeds into BGP blackhole routes: it reads text and JSON feeds, summarises the prefixes and announces them to your routers with a community they can drop on.
