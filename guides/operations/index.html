Guides/Operations

Operations

A first deployment, rolling out changes, telling a refresh from a restart, what healthy logs look like and when to worry.

Day to day, blackhole-threats needs little attention: it refreshes on its own and only touches routes that changed. Most of the work is rolling out config changes safely and noticing when a feed goes bad.

A first deployment

Section titled “A first deployment”
  1. Copy the example config and replace the local ASN, the router ID, the neighbors and the feeds with your own.
  2. Check it with -check-config.
  3. Run it once with -once on a port other than 179, from a host that can reach the feeds, and check the Loaded runtime configuration line shows the local_as, router_id, peer_count and default_community you expect, and that every feed logs Parsed threat feed.
  4. Put the router policy in place before the routes arrive.
  5. Start the long-running service on 179 and watch for the first Refresh completed.
  6. Check on a router that the sessions are up and the routes are installed as blackholes.

Refresh or restart

Section titled “Refresh or restart”

There are two ways to make the daemon act now, and they do different things:

Refresh Restart
How SIGUSR1 or SIGHUP: systemctl reload blackhole-threats, or docker kill --signal=SIGUSR1 systemctl restart blackhole-threats, docker restart
Reads the feeds again Yes Yes, on the first refresh
Reads the YAML file and flags again No Yes
BGP sessions Stay up Close and reconnect; routers drop the routes until the first refresh
Routes kept from failing feeds Yes No, the daemon starts with nothing to carry forward

So refresh when a feed’s contents changed and you don’t want to wait for the timer, and restart for any change to the config or the flags.

Rolling out a config change

Section titled “Rolling out a config change”
  1. Edit a copy of the config.
  2. Check it with -check-config.
  3. If the change adds or removes feeds, changes communities or could change the route count a lot, run the copy with -once on another port and compare the prefixes_read and prefixes_summarized numbers with what the live daemon logs.
  4. If communities changed, update the router policy first.
  5. Put the copy in place and restart the service.
  6. Watch for the first Refresh completed and check its active_routes.

Avoid restarting while a feed is failing if you rely on its routes being carried forward, since a restart loses them.

The refresh interval

Section titled “The refresh interval”

The default is two hours (-refresh-rate 2h). Feeds that change often or matter a lot may warrant something shorter, such as 30m, but most public lists update a few times a day at most, and a short interval mostly means more requests to the providers. -refresh-rate takes Go durations: 90s, 15m, 1h30m.

A shorter interval for a while is handy when you’re investigating a feed. Set it in EXTRA_OPTS or BLACKHOLE_THREATS_EXTRA_OPTS, restart, and put it back afterwards.

What healthy looks like

Section titled “What healthy looks like”

At startup, Loaded runtime configuration with the values you expect. Then on every refresh, one Parsed threat feed per feed, one Prepared community routes per community, and a Refresh completed:

announced and withdrawn are usually small, since only changed routes are sent, and active_routes moves gradually as the feeds change. duration_ms is the whole refresh, which is mostly fetching: communities are read one after another, so it’s roughly the slowest feed in each community added together.

When to look closer

Section titled “When to look closer”
  • The same feed logs Failed to read threat feed on refresh after refresh. Its community is stuck on old routes until it’s fixed.
  • Keeping previous community routes after feed errors keeps appearing for one community.
  • A feed’s prefixes_read falls far below its usual number. A feed that gives no prefixes at all fails and keeps its old routes, but one that gives a handful where it used to give thousands is taken at its word.
  • active_routes jumps or falls sharply after a feed or config change.
  • Dropped prefix shorter than the minimum length from a feed that doesn’t usually list such broad prefixes. Something upstream has probably gone wrong with it.
  • No Refresh completed for longer than the refresh interval.
  • The service keeps restarting with a level=fatal line: the config or a flag is wrong.

Troubleshooting has what to check for each.

blackhole-threats

Turns threat feeds into BGP blackhole routes: it reads text and JSON feeds, summarises the prefixes and announces them to your routers with a community they can drop on.
